# Sky Content Story Bible — Phase 3

## Why this exists

The Phase 2 activity bank reads like worksheets: bare prompts ("What comes
next? red, blue, red, blue, __."), silly distractors ("moon"), no story, no
character voice. Children experience it as a quiz, not an adventure.

Phase 3 rule: **every activity is a moment in an ongoing story.** The child
is a helper and hero, never a test-taker.

## The narrative contract (every single activity)

Each prompt has two parts:

1. **Hook** (one sentence, ~15 words): a story situation starring the host
   character or a story friend. Something happened, someone needs help,
   something is mysterious.
2. **Task** (one sentence): the learning challenge, emerging naturally from
   the hook.

Example — alphabet L2, before and after:

- BEFORE: "Which letter comes right after M?"
- AFTER: "Luna is shelving her star-books in ABC order. She just placed M —
  which letter goes on the shelf next?"

The skill is identical. The feeling is completely different.

## Host voices

Write prompts AS the host speaking to the child. Each host has a voice:

| Host | Island | Voice |
|------|--------|-------|
| Luna (owl) | The Floating Library (reading) | Warm, wise, bookish. "Oh my! The star-books tumbled..." Gentle wonder. |
| Milo (robot) | The Number Volcano (math), The Loom Cloud (coding) | Enthusiastic inventor. "BEEP! My counting machine is stuck..." Playful, loves gadgets. |
| Curio (fox captain) | Inkwell Isle (writing), The Painted Atelier (drawing) | Adventurous, encouraging. "Ahoy, storyteller!..." Treats the child as a fellow adventurer. |
| Bea (bee) | The Greenhouse (science) | Buzzy, nature-loving. "Buzz buzz! Something's sprouting..." Curious about everything alive. |
| Atlas (elephant) | The Observatory (geography) | Grand, warm. "Gather round, explorers..." Makes the world feel big and wonderful. |
| Riff (rabbit) | The Rhythm Stage (music) | Bouncy, rhythmic. "Hop to it!..." Everything is a song waiting to happen. |
| Tuno (turtle) | The Quiet Cloud (feelings) | Slow, calm, kind. "Let's breathe together..." Never rushed, never loud. |
| Nova (kid peer) | Everywhere (try-again moments) | Kid voice. Models mistakes kindly. |

Voice rules:
- Address the child directly ("Can you help me...?", "What do you think...?")
- Never test-speak ("Select the correct answer", "Which of the following")
- Keep total prompt under ~30 words (many players are non-readers; TTS reads it)
- The `narration` field is the speakable version — identical unless the prompt
  references something visual, in which case describe it in words.

## Story friends (recurring characters per subject)

Each subject has 2–4 named friends who recur across skills and levels.
Continuity makes the world feel real. Use them; don't invent a new random
name every activity.

- **Reading**: Pip the mouse (loves cheese, learning to read), Hoot the owlet
  (Luna's chick, curious), the Star-Books (magical books that glow)
- **Math**: Bolt the firefly (counts flashes), Sprocket the mouse inventor
  (builds with Milo), the Number Volcano (rumbles when counted correctly)
- **Writing**: Captain Curio + his parrot Quill, the Inkwell sprites (tiny ink
  creatures who eat letters)
- **Science**: Bea's hive-mates, Sprout the seedling, Dewdrop the frog
- **Geography**: Atlas + his telescope, Wren the traveling bird (sends
  postcards), the globe that spins
- **Coding**: Milo + Sprocket, the Loom sprites (weave patterns)
- **Music**: Riff + his band (Pip on drums, Hoot humming), the Echo Cave
- **Drawing**: Curio + Quill, the Paint sprites (living blobs of color)
- **Feelings**: Tuno + the Calm Cloud, Pebble the crab (learning feelings too)

## Level arcs (the story deepens as levels rise)

- **L1 — "Welcome to the adventure"**: meet the scenario. Simple, playful,
  high success. The host introduces the idea.
- **L2 — "The plot thickens"**: a friend needs help with the same idea, one
  step trickier.
- **L3 — "Help a friend"**: the child solves a problem FOR a story friend.
  The child is the helper.
- **L4 — "The mystery"**: something puzzling happened; the child figures it
  out. Multi-step thinking.
- **L5 — "Master quest"**: the child is the expert — teaching, fixing, or
  leading. Celebratory tone.

## Distractor rules (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

Distractors must be **plausible wrong answers that reveal real misconceptions**.
Never silly, never joke answers.

- Letter order: the letter BEFORE (reversal confusion), skip-one, visually
  similar letter (b/d, p/q)
- Counting: off-by-one, double-counted, counted the wrong set
- Addition: the subtraction answer, digit-sum error, off-by-one
- Patterns: the most recent element repeated, wrong cycle position
- Every multiple_choice has exactly 3 distractors + 1 correct = 4 options
- Distractor labels must be the same KIND of thing as the answer
  (if the answer is a number, distractors are numbers; if a word, words)

BANNED: joke distractors ("moon" for a color, "yelling" as a calm tool when
the other options are serious — actually "yelling" IS a plausible wrong
answer for calm_down; the rule is about category mismatch and absurdity).

## Kind usage (use all 7, each with story purpose)

- `multiple_choice`: the workhorse. Story-framed choice.
- `tap_target`: "find it in the scene" — Luna dropped a book, tap it; find
  the animal hiding in the habitat.
- `tap_count`: count things IN the story — fireflies, honey jars, drum beats.
- `sequence`: repair something broken — put the story events in order, fix
  the ABC shelf, order the rocket countdown.
- `sort`: tidy the story world — sort shells by beach, instruments by family,
  foods by healthy/treat.
- `trace`: draw FOR a character — trace the letter to write Pip a note, trace
  the path to guide the lost firefly home.
- `listen_repeat`: say it WITH a character — blend sounds with Luna, sing
  back with Riff, breathe with Tuno.

## Prompt length & language

- Hook + task ≤ ~30 words total
- Simple words; the CONCEPT can be hard, the LANGUAGE must be easy
- No emoji anywhere children see (use words + art refs)
- Diverse names for story children when needed (Mia, Sam, Leo, Ana, Max, Eva)

## What "interesting" means here

- Surprise: the scenario is delightful, not predictable
- Agency: the child HELPS someone, FIXES something, SOLVES a mystery
- Continuity: friends recur, places persist, jokes land twice
- Voice: you can HEAR Luna/Milo/Bea speaking, not a textbook
